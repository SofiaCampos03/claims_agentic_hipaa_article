The plan's Hospital Plan

Most people get Hospital Plan for free, but some have to pay a premium for this
coverage.

To be eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan, an individual must be entitled to receive

The plan based on their own earnings or those of a spouse, parent, or child. To
receive premium-free Hospital Plan, the worker must have a specified number of quarters of coverage (QCs) and file an application for Social Security or
Railroad Retirement Board (RRB) benefits. The exact number of QCs required is dependent on whether the person is filing for Hospital Plan on the basis of age,
disability, or End Stage Renal Disease (ESRD). QCs are earned through payment of payroll taxes under the Federal Insurance Contributions Act (FICA)
during the person's working years. Most individuals pay the full FICA tax so the QCs they earn can be used to meet the requirements for both monthly
Social Security benefits and premium-free Hospital Plan.

NOTE: Certain Federal, State, and local government employees pay only the
Hospital Plan portion of the FICA tax. The QCs they earn can be used only to meet the

requirements for premium-free Hospital Plan; they may not be used to meet the requirements for monthly Social Security benefits.

Premium-Free the plan's Hospital Plan Based on Age

To be eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan on the basis of age:

A person must be age 65 or older; and

Be eligible for monthly Social Security or Railroad Retirement Board

(RRB) cash benefits.

An individual who is receiving monthly Social Security or RRB benefits, at least

4 months prior to turning age 65, does not need to file a separate application to become entitled to premium-free Hospital Plan.  In this case, the individual will get

Hospital Plan automatically at age 65.

An individual who is not receiving monthly Social Security or RRB benefits
must file an application for the plan by contacting the Social Security
Administration.

Hospital Plan coverage begins the month the individual turns age 65, provided he or
she files an application for Hospital Plan (or for Social Security or RRB benefits)

within 6 months of the month in which he or she becomes age 65. If the
application is filed more than 6 months after turning age 65, Hospital Plan coverage
will be retroactive for 6 months.

NOTE: For an individual whose 65th birthday is on the first day of the month,
Hospital Plan coverage begins on the first day of the month preceding their birth month. For example, if an individual's birthday is on December 1, Hospital Plan begins
on November 1.

The plan's Hospital Plan Based on Age for People Who Must Pay a
Premium

People who must pay a premium for Hospital Plan do not automatically get the plan
when they turn 65. They must:

File an application to enroll by contacting the Social Security

Administration;

Enroll during a valid enrollment period; and

Also enroll in or already have Medical Plan.

To keep premium Hospital Plan, the person must continue to pay all monthly premiums and stay enrolled in Medical Plan. This means that the person must pay both the premium for Medical Plan and the premium for Hospital Plan timely to keep this coverage.

Premium Hospital Plan coverage begins the month following the month of enrollment.

The plan Based on Disability

A person who is entitled to monthly Social Security or Railroad Retirement

Board (RRB) benefits on the basis of disability is automatically entitled to Part

A after receiving disability benefits for 24 months. Disabled federal, state and local government employees who are not eligible for monthly Social Security or RRB benefits may get deemed entitlement to disability benefits and
automatically entitled to Hospital Plan after being disabled for 29 months.

Special Rule for People with Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) (Lou
Gehrig’s disease)

Individuals whose disability is Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) are entitled
to Hospital Plan the first month they are entitled to Social Security or RRB disability
cash benefits. There is no waiting period.

Special Rule for People Claiming Child Disability Benefits

SSA rules do not allow for child disability benefits to begin earlier than age 18.
Therefore, Hospital Plan entitlement based on child disability benefit entitlement can
never begin before the month the person attains age 20 (or age 18 if the
individual’s disability is ALS).

The plan Based on End-Stage Renal Disease (ESRD)

Individuals are eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan if they receive regular dialysis
treatments or a kidney transplant, have filed an application for the plan, and
meet one of the following conditions:

Have worked the required amount of time under Social Security, the
Railroad Retirement Board (RRB), or as a government employee; or

Are getting or are eligible for Social Security or RRB benefits; or

Are the spouse or dependent child of a person who has worked the required amount of time under Social Security, the RRB, or as a government employee; or are getting Social Security or RRB benefits.

Hospital Plan coverage begins:

The 3rd month after the month in which a regular course of dialysis begins; or

The first month a regular course of dialysis begins if the individual engages in self-dialysis training; or

The month of kidney transplant; or

Two months prior to the month of transplant if the individual was hospitalized during those months in preparation for the transplant

The plan's Medical Plan (Medical Insurance)

Individuals already receiving Social Security or RRB benefits at least 4 months
before being eligible for the plan and residing in the United States (except
residents of Puerto Rico) are automatically enrolled in both premium-free Part
A and Medical Plan. People who are automatically enrolled have the choice of
whether they want to keep or refuse Medical Plan coverage. People living in Puerto
Rico who are eligible for automatic enrollment are only enrolled in premium-
free Hospital Plan; they must actively enroll in Medical Plan to get this coverage.

Individuals who are not receiving a Social Security or RRB benefit are not
automatically enrolled. Individuals who previously refused Medical Plan, or who
terminated their Medical Plan enrollment, may enroll (or re-enroll) in Medical Plan only
during certain enrollment periods. In most cases, if someone does not enroll in
Medical Plan when first eligible, they will have to pay a late enrollment penalty for as long as they have Medical Plan.

Medical Plan is a voluntary program that requires the payment of a monthly premium
for all parts of coverage. Eligibility rules for Medical Plan depend on whether a
person is eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan or whether the individual has to pay
a premium for Hospital Plan coverage.

Individuals who are eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan are also eligible for enroll
in Medical Plan once they are entitled to Hospital Plan.

Individuals who must pay a premium for Hospital Plan must meet the following requirements to enroll in Medical Plan:

Be age 65 or older;

Be a U.S. resident; AND

Be either a U.S. citizen, OR

Be an alien who has been lawfully admitted for permanent residence and has been residing in the United States for 5 continuous years prior to the month of filing an application for the plan.

NOTE: Individuals who were terminated from Hospital Plan 36 months after receiving a kidney transplant may be eligible for the Medical Plan Immunosuppressive Drug benefit. Learn more about Medical Plan Immunosuppressive Drug Coverage.

Enrollment Periods and When Coverage Begins

Individuals eligible for premium-free Hospital Plan, who are not automatically
enrolled, can enroll in Hospital Plan at any time after they are first eligible for the
coverage.

Individuals who want premium Hospital Plan, Medical Plan or both may only enroll during
certain enrollment periods that are outlined in law. The following enrollment
periods apply to both premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan:

Initial Enrollment Period;

General Enrollment Period;

Special Enrollment Period for

Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

The IEP is a 7-month period that begins 3 months before the month a person turns 65, their birthday month and ends 3 months after the person turns 65.
For someone under age 65 who becomes entitled to the plan based on
disability, entitlement begins with the 25  month of disability benefit
entitlement. For these individuals, the IEP begins 3 months before the 25
month of disability benefit entitlement, includes the 25 month, and ends
three months after. The IEP for people with ESRD and ALS varies based on
their situation.

th th th

Coverage will begin the month after a person enrolls during their IEP. Disabled individuals are automatically enrolled in the plan's Hospital Plan and Medical Plan after they have received disability benefits from Social Security for 24 months.

NOTE: In most cases, if someone does not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible, they will have to pay a late enrollment penalty. The Medical Plan penalty is assessed for as long as the person has Medical Plan.

General Enrollment Period (GEP)

The GEP is a 3-month period that takes place from January 1 through March 31 of each year. Medical Plan and premium Hospital Plan coverage will begin the month after a person enrolls during the GEP.

Special Enrollment Period (SEP)

There are certain situations when a person can sign up for Medical Plan (and
Premium Hospital Plan) during a Special Enrollment Period without paying a late
enrollment penalty. A Special Enrollment Period is only available for a limited
time. If the person doesn’t sign up during their Special Enrollment Period,
they’ll have to wait for the next General Enrollment Period and they might have
to pay a monthly late enrollment penalty.

Coverage will begin the month after a person enrolls during their SEP.

SEP for the Working Aged and Working Disabled

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible
because they were covered under a group health plan based on their own or a
spouse's current employment (or the current employment of a family member,
if disabled) may enroll during this SEP.

The individual can enroll at any time while covered under the group health plan based on current employment, or during the 8-month period that begins the
month the employment ends or the group health plan coverage ends,
whichever comes first.

NOTE: Individuals with ESRD are not eligible to enroll using this SEP.

SEP for International Volunteers

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible because they were performing volunteer service outside of the United States for at least 12 months on behalf of a tax-exempt organization and had health insurance that provided coverage for the duration of the volunteer service may enroll using this SEP.

The SEP is a 6-month period that begins the earlier of the first day of the month following the month for which the:

individual was no longer serving as a volunteer outside of the United

States;

organization no longer has tax-exempt status; or individual no longer has health insurance that provides coverage outside of the United States.

Special Enrollment Period for Exceptional Conditions

Effective January 1, 2023, the following SEPs for exceptional conditions are
available for premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan, respectively:

SEP for Individuals Impacted by an Emergency or Disaster

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible due to having an emergency or disaster declared by a federal, state, or local
government entity in their area may enroll using this SEP.

If a person is eligible because they were impacted by an emergency or
disaster, they can sign up as early as the month of the emergency or disaster,
or up to six months after the emergency or disaster has ended. They may also be able to use this SEP if the disaster or emergency takes place where their
authorized representative, legal guardian, or person who makes health care
decisions on their behalf resides.

The SEP begins the date an emergency or disaster is declared, or the start date identified in the emergency declaration, whichever is earlier. The SEP
ends six months after the later of:

The end date identified in the disaster or emergency declaration, or

The end date of any extensions or the date when the declaration has been
determined to have ended or has been revoked, or

The date of the declaration, if such date is after the end of the disaster.

The plan benefits will be effective the first day of the month following enrollment.

SEP for Health Plan or Employer Misrepresentation

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible due to misrepresentation or reliance on incorrect information provided by their employer or group health plan (GHP), agents or brokers of health plans, or any person authorized to act on behalf of such entity may enroll using this SEP.

An eligible person must demonstrate (by documentation or written attestation)

both of the following:

They did not enroll in part B or premium Hospital Plan during an enrollment period in which they were eligible based on information received from an employer or GHP, agents or brokers of health plans, or any person
authorized to act on such organization’s behalf.

An employer, GHP, agent or broker of a health plan, or their representative
materially misrepresented information or provided incorrect information
relating to enrollment in part B or premium Hospital Plan.

This SEP begins the day an individual notifies the Social Security
Administration and ends six months later.

The plan benefits will be effective the first day of the month following
enrollment.

SEP for Formerly Incarcerated Individuals

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible
because they were incarcerated may enroll using this SEP.

If a person is eligible because they are not enrolled in the plan due to being
incarcerated, they can sign up anytime within the first 12 months after their release from incarceration.

This SEP begins the day an individual is released from the custody of
penal authorities and ends the last day of the 12  month after that
release.

th

Individuals have the option to select a retroactive effective date (not to
begin prior to their release date and not to exceed 6 months). If

individuals choose this retroactive option, they will be responsible for paying the plan premiums back to the date of coverage.

The plan benefits will be effective the first day of the month following enrollment, or up to 6 months retroactive, if the beneficiary, chooses retroactive coverage.

For additional information please visit your insurer's member portal education/look-up-topics/special-populations/incarcerated-medicare- beneficiaries.

SEP to Coordinate with Termination of the state medical assistance program Coverage

Individuals whose the state medical assistance program eligibility terminated may enroll using this SEP.

A person may be eligible if they have lost the state medical assistance program entirely, missed a plan enrollment period and their the state medical assistance program coverage was terminated on or after
January 1, 2023.

The SEP begins when an individual is notified of an upcoming termination
of the state medical assistance program eligibility and ends six months after the state medical assistance program
termination.

The plan benefits start the month after the plan enrollment unless the
individual elects a start date back to the first day of the month the
individual lost the state medical assistance program and agrees to pay all prior premiums.

NOTE: Individuals who still are eligible for the state medical assistance program, including a plan
Savings Program, and have not received notice of an upcoming the state medical assistance program
termination are not eligible for this SEP.

SEP for Other Exceptional Conditions

Individuals who do not enroll in Medical Plan or premium Hospital Plan when first eligible
because conditions beyond their control caused them to miss an enrollment
period may enroll using this SEP. This SEP is available for individuals whose
unique conditions do not qualify for other SEPs. An eligible person must
demonstrate (by documentation or written attestation) that conditions outside
of their control that occurred on or after January 1, 2023, caused them to miss
an enrollment period.

An individual’s request for this SEP will only be granted in conditions that are
truly exceptional in nature, and will not be used to grant individual’s

enrollment due to forgetfulness, lack of knowledge, or failure to make premium payments.

SSA will determine when this SEP begins on a case-by-case basis, but the SEP will end no less than 6 months after it begins. The plan benefits will be effective the first day of the month following enrollment.

Termination of Enrollment

Individuals entitled to premium-free Hospital Plan cannot voluntarily terminate their
Hospital Plan coverage. This is not permitted by law. Generally, premium-free Hospital Plan ends due to:

Loss of entitlement to Social Security or Railroad Retirement Board
benefits; or

Death.

There are special rules for when premium-free Hospital Plan ends for people with

ESRD.

Premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan coverage can be voluntarily terminated because
premium payments are required. Premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan coverage ends due to:

Voluntary disenrollment request (coverage ends prospectively);

Failure to pay premiums;

For individuals under age 65 (disabled and ESRD), loss of Hospital Plan
entitlement (Medical Plan terminates at the same time as Hospital Plan); or

Death.

For individuals who want to disenroll from Premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan
coverage, use form the plan administrator-1763 in the downloads section of this page.

Premiums

Premium Hospital Plan and Medical Plan coverage requires payment of monthly premiums.

IRMAA (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount)

Individuals and married couples with an income over a certain limit must pay a higher premium for Medical Plan and an extra amount for Drug Plan coverage in addition
to their Drug Plan plan premium. This additional amount is called income-related monthly adjustment amount. Less than 5 percent of people with the plan are affected, so most people will not pay a higher premium. Visit the plan
Parts A & B Income Related Adjustment Amounts page for information about income limits.

Late Enrollment Penalty (LEP) for Premium Hospital Plan

If an individual did not enroll in premium Hospital Plan when first eligible, they may have to pay a higher monthly premium if they decide to enroll later. The
monthly premium for Hospital Plan may increase up to 10%. The individual will have to pay the higher premium for twice the number of years the individual could
have had Hospital Plan but did not sign up.

For individuals enrolling using the SEP for the Working Aged and Working
Disabled, the premium Hospital Plan LEP is calculated by adding the months that have elapsed between the close of the individual’s IEP and the end of the
month in which the individual enrolls. For enrollments after your IEP has ended, months where you had group health plan coverage are excluded from
the LEP calculation.

For individuals enrolling using an Exceptional Conditions SEP or the

International Volunteers SEP, no LEP will be applied.

Late Enrollment Penalty (LEP) for Medical Plan

If an individual did not sign up for Medical Plan when first eligible, the individual may have to pay a late enrollment penalty for as long as the individual has
The plan. The individual’s monthly premium for Medical Plan may go up 10% for each full 12-month period that the individual could have had Medical Plan but did not sign
up for it.

For individuals enrolling using the SEP for the Working Aged and Working

Disabled, the Medical Plan LEP is calculated by adding the months that have elapsed
between the close of the individual’s IEP and the end of the month in which the individual enrolls. For enrollments after your IEP has ended, months where you
had group health plan coverage are excluded from the LEP calculation.

For individuals enrolling using an Exceptional Conditions SEP, the International

Volunteers SEP, or the SEP for Certain TRICARE Beneficiaries, no LEP will be
applied.
